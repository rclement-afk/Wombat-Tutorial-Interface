Mechanical Challenges

Two foundation challenges that carry the philosophy, twenty-six build challenges — each a mechanism you construct, test, and measure — and two open design projects at the end. Build sheets follow the same seven phases — observe, learn the concept, build, meet a need, test and iterate, connect to Botball, and reflect. Enter your PIN, work through the activity, then press Submit & Download to save your results file and a printable PDF.

Design Foundations · Challenges 1–2

Challenge 1 Design Foundations What engineering actually is, the five-step design process, needs versus constraints — the philosophy every build here runs on. Nothing to build yet. Open Challenge → Challenge 2 How Gears Work Teeth, mesh, direction, ratio, torque, and compound gearing — the language every mechanism from Challenge 3 onward speaks. Open Challenge →

Effectors · Challenges 3–5

Challenge 3 The Pincer Meshed gears reverse direction. Build a grabber where both arms close together instead of one shoving the object sideways. Open Challenge → Challenge 4 The Modified Pincer Gear ratios trade speed for torque, and swinging jaws tilt against a flat face. Gears are not magic. Open Challenge → Challenge 5 The Big Arm Degrees of freedom, torque measured in oz-in, and counterweights — all under a 120 oz-in ceiling. Open Challenge →

Converting Motion · Challenges 6–8

Challenge 6 The Crank Rotation into straight-line motion. Stroke is twice the pin offset, and dead points stop everything. Open Challenge → Challenge 7 The Cam The profile decides the motion. Lift, the pause between, and the return force that keeps the follower down. Open Challenge → Challenge 8 The Four-Bar Linkage Four links, one of them invisible, and an arithmetic test that decides whether the motor will spin or jam. Open Challenge →

Gears and Drives · Challenges 9–14

Challenge 9 The Worm Gear 24:1 from two parts, a right-angle turn, and a drive that holds its position with the power off. Open Challenge → Challenge 10 The Worm-Driven Pincer One worm drives two gears in opposite directions — and the grip stays clamped after the motor stops. Open Challenge → Challenge 11 The Rack and Pinion A jaw that slides cannot tilt. Prove the jaws are parallel by measuring, not by looking. Open Challenge → Challenge 12 The Coulisse Mechanism A pin riding in a slot makes one stroke fast and the other slow, from a wheel turning at a steady rate. Open Challenge → Challenge 13 The Bevel Drive A second way to turn a corner, and why a worm gear would ruin this one. The first build that drives. Open Challenge → Challenge 14 The Geneva Drive Four exact stops every minute, locked in place by shape between each one. Dwell is the useful part. Open Challenge →

Midpoint Synthesis · Challenge 15

Challenge 15 Combining Mechanisms Halfway. Chain at least two of the nine mechanisms you have built and produce more than six inches of travel. Open Challenge →

Reaching and Lifting · Challenges 16–20

Challenge 16 Planar Motion with Three Inputs A flat object has exactly three degrees of freedom, so it takes exactly three drives. Parallel machines versus serial ones. Open Challenge → Challenge 17 The Vertical Transitioning Platform Two pounds, five inches, one motor. Mechanical advantage gets its name here — and its price. Open Challenge → Challenge 18 The Four-Bar Linkage Crane Mechanical advantage is not a single number. Find the hardest position and size the motor for that one. Open Challenge → Challenge 19 Drive at 45 Degrees Gears are cut for one angle. A joint bends to any angle — and gives up a perfectly even output to do it. Open Challenge → Challenge 20 Using Linear Teeth to Increase Length Sliding stages add their reach together. Reaching past your own base is bought with stability. Open Challenge →

Control and Precision · Challenges 21–27

Challenge 21 The Parallelogram Elevator Up and down from a motor that never reverses. Level platform or straight path — you cannot easily have both. Open Challenge → Challenge 22 The Sliding Platform Most of the work in a slide is preventing motion, not creating it. Skew, guides, and binding. Open Challenge → Challenge 23 Gear and Linkage Mechanism Park the effector, cut the power, and drag the load. Then find out whether it held because it locks or because it is stiff. Open Challenge → Challenge 24 One Motor, Two Speeds Split a drive train and each branch gets its own gearing. A striker spinning fast while the car crawls forward. Open Challenge → Challenge 25 The String-Driven Extension Arm A string can only pull. Half the mechanism is missing, and supplying it is the challenge. Open Challenge → Challenge 26 The Shaft Rotation Limiter A mechanism built to stop. Hand crank only — and understanding exactly why is most of the lesson. Open Challenge → Challenge 27 Keeping Direction the Same While in Rotation A Ferris wheel whose platforms never tip, held level by a sprocket at the centre that never turns. Open Challenge →
